# Starter social plan: @northvalelearn

Fifteen posts to start with. Each has the picture or clip, a caption in English and one in Spanish, and a note on what to check before you post.

The handle `@northvalelearn` was free everywhere when we checked (see `docs/BRAND-CHECK.md`), but it isn't yours until you claim it. Claim it on each app before anything else.

## The voice

Calm, plain and kind. Like a good older cousin who happens to know about money.

- Short sentences. No hype, no "you won't believe", no countdowns, no "get rich".
- At most one emoji per post, and none is fine. The boat ⛵ is the one that fits.
- Never tell anyone what to buy, sell or open. Explain, then let them choose.
- Write the English and the Spanish separately, in the same calm voice. Don't run one through a translator. Use Mexican Spanish (tú, pesos, "celular").
- Every post can end with the small line: *Educational only. Not financial advice.* / *Solo con fines educativos. No es asesoría financiera.*

## Rules that keep the posts honest

1. No made-up numbers, user counts, testimonials, reviews or "trusted by". If it isn't true today, it doesn't go in.
2. Any fact about money (a rule, a limit, a fee) gets checked against an official source the week you post it. Rules change. The "Check first" line on each post says what to look at.
3. For "Explain this" posts, use the real app: ask the question, screenshot the real answer, and read it before you post. The explanations in this file are the idea of the post, not a recording of what the app said.
4. Never show a real person's account, balance, name or email. Use a fresh test profile when you record or screenshot.
5. No giveaways, no paid shout-outs. The promise says nobody pays us to recommend anything, and posts shouldn't look like they do.
6. Answer comments in the language they were written in. Never ask anyone to send account numbers, codes or passwords, and never give personal advice in replies or DMs. "I can't give personal advice, but here's how it works…" is enough.

## Rhythm

Three posts a week is plenty to start. A simple loop: one myth, one border tip, then an "Explain this" or an app moment. Post the English and Spanish versions as two posts on the same day, or as one post with a Spanish line under the English, whichever feels better on that app.

# Money myths (4 posts)

### 1. "Checking your credit score lowers it"

- **Picture:** A plain cream card, navy text: *Myth: checking your own credit hurts it.* Second card: *Not so.*
- **Caption (EN):** Myth: looking at your own credit score lowers it. In the US, checking your own score is a "soft" look and doesn't hurt it. In Mexico, you can ask Buró de Crédito for your own report free once a year. Knowing where you stand is the first step. Save this for later. ⛵
- **Caption (ES):** Mito: ver tu propio puntaje de crédito lo baja. En Estados Unidos, revisar tu propio puntaje es una consulta "suave" y no lo daña. En México, puedes pedirle a Buró de Crédito tu propio reporte gratis una vez al año. Saber dónde estás es el primer paso. Guárdalo para después. ⛵
- **Check first:** the free-report rules on the Buró de Crédito and annualcreditreport.com pages.

### 2. "A debit card builds credit"

- **Picture:** Two cards side by side: *Debit: your own money. Credit: borrowed money.* Caption text on the picture: *Only borrowing gets reported.*
- **Caption (EN):** Myth: using a debit card builds your credit. A debit card spends your own money, so there's no loan to report. Credit history comes from borrowing, like a credit card, and paying it back on time. Neither one is "better". They do different jobs.
- **Caption (ES):** Mito: usar una tarjeta de débito construye tu crédito. Con débito gastas tu propio dinero, así que no hay un préstamo que reportar. El historial de crédito viene de pedir prestado, como con una tarjeta de crédito, y pagar a tiempo. Ninguna es "mejor". Hacen trabajos distintos.
- **Check first:** that your examples say "usually" if you add details about specific banks. Some bank products blur the line.

### 3. "Paying the minimum is fine"

- **Picture:** A short carousel. Card 1: *The minimum keeps your account in good standing.* Card 2: *It's small on purpose.* Card 3: *Most of it can go to interest.* Card 4: *Pay more than the minimum when you can.*
- **Caption (EN):** The minimum payment keeps your card account in good standing. It's built to be small, so a lot of it can go to interest and the balance barely moves. When you can, pay more than the minimum. Even a little more helps.
- **Caption (ES):** El pago mínimo mantiene tu tarjeta al corriente. Es pequeño a propósito, así que gran parte puede ir a intereses y el saldo casi no baja. Cuando puedas, paga más que el mínimo. Aunque sea un poco más, ayuda.
- **Check first:** nothing numeric. If you add an example with numbers, calculate it yourself and say it's made up.

### 4. "You need a lot of money to learn about investing"

- **Picture:** A screen recording of the practice portfolio, from the app.
- **Caption (EN):** You don't need a lot of money to start learning. In Northvale Learn you practice with pretend money: $10,000, or $200,000 pesos. No real money moves. Make a choice, see what happens, learn from it.
- **Caption (ES):** No necesitas mucho dinero para empezar a aprender. En Northvale Learn practicas con dinero de práctica: $200,000 pesos, o $10,000 dólares. No se mueve dinero real. Toma una decisión, mira qué pasa y aprende.
- **Check first:** the starting amounts shown in the portfolio screen when you record.

# Border money tips (4 posts)

### 5. "Pay in the local currency"

- **Picture:** A card terminal drawing, two buttons: *Pay in pesos* / *Pay in dollars*. A small check by the local one.
- **Caption (EN):** At a card machine or ATM in the other country, you may be asked: pay in your home currency or the local one? The offer to convert it for you at checkout usually comes with a worse exchange rate. Choosing the local currency is usually the better deal.
- **Caption (ES):** En una terminal de tarjeta o un cajero del otro país, a veces te preguntan: ¿pagas en tu moneda o en la moneda local? Que te la conviertan en ese momento suele traer un tipo de cambio peor. Elegir la moneda local suele convenir más.
- **Check first:** that the wording says "usually". Look at your own card's foreign-fee terms and mention them if you want to go further.

### 6. "Ask for the total cost, not just the fee"

- **Picture:** A small sum: *Fee + exchange rate = what really arrives.*
- **Caption (EN):** Sending money across the border? A low fee isn't the whole story. The exchange rate matters too. Compare how many pesos arrive for each dollar, then add the fee. Profeco publishes a comparison of money-transfer services to help.
- **Caption (ES):** ¿Vas a enviar dinero al otro lado de la frontera? Una comisión baja no es toda la historia. También importa el tipo de cambio. Compara cuántos pesos llegan por cada dólar y suma la comisión. Profeco publica un comparativo de servicios de envío de dinero para ayudarte.
- **Check first:** that Profeco's money-transfer comparison ("Quién es quién en el envío de dinero") is still published, and link to its current page.

### 7. "Two countries, two credit histories"

- **Picture:** Two folders, one labeled *US credit report* and one *Buró de Crédito*. (No flags: keep it simple and neutral.)
- **Caption (EN):** A US credit history and a Mexican one are kept separately. Moving or working on one side doesn't automatically carry your history to the other. If your life crosses the border, start small, pay on time and keep your own records.
- **Caption (ES):** El historial de crédito de Estados Unidos y el de México se llevan por separado. Mudarte o trabajar de un lado no pasa automáticamente tu historial al otro. Si tu vida cruza la frontera, empieza poco a poco, paga a tiempo y guarda tus propios registros.
- **Check first:** whether any new cross-border credit program has appeared. If so, don't say "never", keep "doesn't automatically".

### 8. "FDIC and IPAB"

- **Picture:** Two short cards, *FDIC (US)* and *IPAB (Mexico)*, with *Both have limits.*
- **Caption (EN):** Before you open an account, ask who protects the money. In the US, banks are usually covered by the FDIC. In Mexico, it's the IPAB. Both have limits, so check the current ones for the account you're looking at.
- **Caption (ES):** Antes de abrir una cuenta, pregunta quién protege el dinero. En Estados Unidos, los bancos suelen estar cubiertos por la FDIC. En México, por el IPAB. Los dos tienen límites, así que revisa los vigentes para la cuenta que estás viendo.
- **Check first:** the current limits on fdic.gov and ipab.org.mx if you decide to state any numbers. The captions above deliberately don't.

# "Explain this" examples (4 posts)

Use the real app for each one. Ask, screenshot the real answer, and read it before you post. If the app's answer isn't right or clear, don't post it. Report it through the feedback button and fix the app first. The caption is the idea, not a quote of what the app said.

### 9. APR and CAT

- **Picture:** The app's answer to *Explain this: APR* (or *CAT*), screenshotted.
- **Caption (EN):** Stuck on a word? Ask. Today's: APR, the yearly cost of borrowing, shown as a percentage. In Mexico the same idea is called CAT (Costo Anual Total). A lower number usually means a cheaper loan, so compare them. Explained in plain words, and never a tip on what to buy.
- **Caption (ES):** ¿Te atoraste con una palabra? Pregunta. La de hoy: CAT (Costo Anual Total), lo que cuesta un crédito en un año, mostrado como porcentaje. En Estados Unidos se llama APR. Un número menor suele significar un crédito más barato, así que compáralos. Explicado con palabras sencillas, y nunca un consejo de qué comprar.
- **Check first:** that the app's own answer matches the caption. Don't promise "a lower number is always cheaper".

### 10. A headline

- **Picture:** A headline you write yourself, like *The central bank holds interest rates steady*, with the app's answer under it.
- **Caption (EN):** Headlines can sound like they're in another language. *"The central bank holds interest rates steady"* means it left a key interest rate where it was. That can nudge what banks charge for loans and pay on savings, but not overnight. Paste a headline into Northvale Learn and ask it to explain.
- **Caption (ES):** Los titulares a veces suenan como otro idioma. *"El banco central mantiene sin cambios la tasa de interés"* quiere decir que dejó una tasa importante donde estaba. Eso puede influir en lo que cobran los bancos por los créditos y lo que pagan por el ahorro, pero no de un día para otro. Pega un titular en Northvale Learn y pídele que te lo explique.
- **Check first:** write your own headline, so you're not copying a news outlet's. Don't name a real outlet or a specific decision.

### 11. "Remesa" and "tipo de cambio"

- **Picture:** The app's answer to *Explain this: remittance / remesa*.
- **Caption (EN):** A remittance, or *remesa*, is money sent to family in another country. Two things set what arrives: the fee, and the exchange rate. Learning both words makes any transfer easier to compare.
- **Caption (ES):** Una remesa es dinero que se envía a la familia en otro país. Dos cosas deciden cuánto llega: la comisión y el tipo de cambio. Conocer las dos palabras hace más fácil comparar cualquier envío.
- **Check first:** the app's answer, as in every "Explain this" post.

### 12. Compound interest

- **Picture:** A simple growing-bar graphic you make, labeled *an example with made-up numbers*.
- **Caption (EN):** Compound interest is interest that earns interest. The longer money stays put, the more the growth builds on itself. The example in the picture uses made-up numbers. It's there to show the idea, not to promise anything. Ask Northvale Learn to explain it in your own words.
- **Caption (ES):** El interés compuesto es el interés que gana intereses. Mientras más tiempo se queda el dinero, más crece sobre lo que ya creció. El ejemplo de la imagen usa números inventados: sirve para mostrar la idea, no para prometer nada. Pídele a Northvale Learn que te lo explique con tus palabras.
- **Check first:** that the picture says "made-up numbers" in both languages.

## What to do when someone asks for something you don't have

If someone asks for a testimonial, a number of users, or a comparison with another app, the honest answer is the small one: *"It's a small beta, and we'd rather show you than tell you."* Don't make something up. When real people give feedback and say you may quote them, ask them in writing first, and quote only their exact words.
