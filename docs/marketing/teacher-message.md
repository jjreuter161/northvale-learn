# A message for teachers

Send this from your own email or messages. Fill in the three `[brackets]`. Attach `pdf/flyer-en.pdf` (or `flyer-es.pdf`).

Why it's written this way: teachers get a lot of mail, and this one involves students under 18. So it's short, it says exactly what the app is and isn't, and it asks for one small thing: a look and honest feedback. It doesn't ask them to put the app in front of a class yet, because classroom mode is still a test with invited teachers and the terms are still being reviewed.

---

## English

**Subject:** A free, bilingual money app made by a student (a look, not a commitment)

Hi [Teacher's name],

I'm a student, and I'm building Northvale Learn: a free app that teaches how money works in four-minute lessons, in English or Spanish, with pretend money only. It's made for young people whose lives cross the US–Mexico border, and it's useful to anyone 13 and up.

There are no ads, we never sell data, and nobody pays us to recommend anything. It isn't a bank, and it doesn't give advice.

It's a small beta. I'd love it if you took five minutes to look at it and told me what's confusing or missing. If it seems useful for a class or club, there's a classroom mode I'm testing with a few invited teachers. It isn't open to everyone yet, so please get your school's OK before using anything with students.

The link is [link], and the flyer is attached.

Thank you for your time,
[Your first name]

## If they write back with questions

Answer with these. They're all true today; if something changes, change the answer.

| They ask | You say |
|---|---|
| Does it cost anything? | No. It's a free beta. |
| What happens with student data? | There are no ads and we never sell data. Students in a class join with a class code and a nickname, no email. Teachers see lesson progress, never goals, amounts or notes. The full text is in the privacy policy. |
| Is it safe for under-18s? | It's for ages 13 and up. Making an account needs a parent's or guardian's OK, teens only compete with other teens, and classroom mode has no messaging of any kind. |
| Does it give financial advice? | No. Lessons explain how money works and never say what to buy or sell. The AI tutor explains terms in plain words and never tells anyone what to buy. |
| Can I use it with my whole class? | Not yet. Classroom mode is a test with invited teachers while the terms are reviewed. I'll write when it's ready. |
| Who are you? | A student. (Say as much or as little as you like. Nothing in the kit asks you to share your last name, age, school or photo.) |
