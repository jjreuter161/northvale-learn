## Where Northvale wins

1. **Independent.** No bank, card or sponsor stands behind us, so the lessons never sell anything. Zogo, Greenlight, GoHenry and BBVA all teach next to a financial product. "Our promise" says so, and it's true in the code.
2. **Built for both countries, in both languages.** Lessons, numbers and tools exist for the US and for Mexico (CETES, AFORE, aguinaldo, IVA, RFC…). Everyone above serves one country. Zogo has Spanish, but its lessons follow US standards.
3. **Learning by doing, safely.** Pretend-money practice, real goals, calculators and real-life missions, not just quizzes, and no real card needed.
4. **A daily habit that respects people.** Short lessons, reviews that come back, a calm streak, and no hearts or lives taking away your turn.
5. **Free for learners.**

**Where we're behind:**
- **Size:** Zogo has 1,200+ modules and Khan has 16 deep units; we have 59 lessons.
- **Real money:** Greenlight and GoHenry let teens spend for real.
- **Reach:** Zogo is sold through banks, and Khan and NGPF are in schools.
- **No rewards:** we have no marketplace or gift cards.
- **No store app** yet.

## Two things to act on

- **"Money Missions" is GoHenry's name for its lessons** (since 2021). BBVA México also calls its teen challenges "misiones." Using the same name invites confusion, and maybe a trademark problem. **Recommendation:** rename ours before the public launch. Options: "Real-life missions" / "Misiones de la vida real", or "Try it for real" / "Pruébalo de verdad", which the finish screen already uses. The lawyer should check whichever we pick.
- **For money transfers, the official comparison already exists.** Profeco's "Quién es Quién en el Envío de Dinero" compares real companies' fees and rates by city. Our remittance tool should teach *how* fees and exchange markups add up, with example numbers, then link to Profeco's table for real prices. That keeps us from naming or ranking companies, as planned.
