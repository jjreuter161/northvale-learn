# Beta plan: 20–50 testers, one question

**The goal:** find out whether people come back the next day, and whether at least about 40% would be **"very disappointed"** without Northvale. Everything else is secondary.

## Who to invite, and where

**Aim for 30–50 people,** because about half of invites turn into real use.

1. **Friends and classmates, 18–25** (about 15). They're the easiest to reach and honest. Use group chats and class Discord or WhatsApp groups.
2. **Family and friends' younger siblings, 13–17** (about 10). **Get a parent's OK first**, in writing (a text message is fine). The app also asks them to confirm. Tell parents it's pretend money and educational, and show them the privacy page.
3. **School clubs.** Business, economics, investing or DECA clubs; ask the club advisor first. In Mexico: a school's "club de finanzas" or "emprendimiento".
4. **Online communities for young investors** (about 10–20). Use communities that allow sharing projects: an r/teenagers or r/personalfinance weekly self-promotion thread (follow each sub's rules), Discord servers for student investors, and in Mexico, groups like "Finanzas para jóvenes". Ask moderators first. Never spam.
5. **Spanish speakers on purpose.** Get at least 5–10 Spanish-speaking testers so the Mexico content is really tested.

Keep the beta closed: set `SIGNUP_CODE` and give the code only in your invite. People can still use the app without an account; the code only gates accounts.

## What to tell testers

- It's a 5-minute-a-day app that teaches money and investing with pretend money.
- Try it for **one week**, ideally a little every day.
- Nothing is real money. Nothing is financial advice.
- **The bug reporting:** tap **"Send feedback"** at the bottom of any screen. In a lesson, the flag icon reports that exact step.
- After a week you'll send them a few questions (5 minutes).

## What to ask after a week (5 minutes)

1. How would you feel if you could no longer use Northvale? (Very / Somewhat / Not disappointed). The app asks this after lesson 3; ask again here.
2. What's the main benefit you get from it?
3. What type of person do you think would benefit most from it?
4. How can we improve it for you?
5. What almost made you stop using it?
6. Was anything confusing or wrong? (lessons, the portfolio, the tutor)
7. Did you use the AI tutor? Was it helpful? Did it ever say something odd?
8. Would you tell a friend about it? Why or why not?

Questions 1–4 are the standard "product–market fit" survey. Group the answers to question 2 by the people who said "very disappointed"; that's what to build more of.

## Reading the numbers (/admin)

- **Day-1 return.** 30%+ is decent for a brand-new app. Under 20% means the first lesson or the hook needs work.
- **Day-7 return.** 15%+ is a good early sign.
- **Where people stop.** A step where many people stop is the first thing to rewrite.
- **"Very disappointed."** 40%+ is a strong signal. Under 25% means listen hard to the "somewhat" group: what would make it a must-have for them?

## One-paragraph description

> **Northvale Learn** teaches money and investing in five minutes a day. Short lessons explain budgeting, saving, credit, compound interest, stocks, index funds, risk and retirement in plain words — then you practice with a pretend $10,000 portfolio, plan goals in a simulator, and ask an AI tutor about anything you don't get. It's in English and Spanish, with real examples for the US and Mexico. It's educational only: no real money, no real trades, and nothing to sell you.
