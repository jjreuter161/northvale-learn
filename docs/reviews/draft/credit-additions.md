## Debt & credit (added lessons)

## Lessons

### Reading a credit card statement

*In real life:* Find the due date and the minimum payment on a real statement, yours or a family member's.  
*4.0 min*

**Card: Four boxes**

> Priya opens her card statement. Four things matter first: the due date, the minimum payment, the interest charged this month and the APR. Which payment keeps her from being charged interest next month?

**Question (choice, `read_statement`):** Which payment avoids interest?

- ✅ The full balance, by the due date
- ◻️ The minimum, on time
  - *If picked:* The minimum keeps you current, but interest builds.
- ◻️ Any amount, paid late
  - *If picked:* Paying late can add fees and interest.

*Why:* Paying the whole balance by the due date avoids interest.

**Card: The minimum isn't the goal**

> Issuers must show how long paying only the minimum would take. The minimum covers mostly interest and a little of the balance. The APR is the yearly rate on purchases, and paying late can bring fees.

**Question (number, `read_statement`):** A $1,000 balance with no payments for a year, at the typical card rate of 20%. About how much interest?

- Answer: **200**
- Steps: 20% of $1,000. → 0.2 × $1,000 = $200.

**Question (match, `read_statement`):** Match each line to what it tells you.

- Due date = The last day to pay on time
- Minimum payment = The least to stay current, with interest still building
- Interest charged = What the card cost you this month

*Why:* Each line answers a different question about the bill.

**Action** (`goals.debt`): Compare paying the minimum vs. paying double on a card balance.

### When borrowing makes sense

*In real life:* Before you borrow for something, write its total cost and what waiting would cost.  
*4.4 min*

**Card: A laptop for school**

> Sam needs a $600 laptop for school. A loan would let him pay it over 12 months, with $60 of interest. Or he could wait six months and pay cash. Is borrowing worth it? It depends on what waiting costs him.

**Question (number, `borrow_test`):** The loan's total is the price plus the interest. How much will Sam pay in all?

- Answer: **660**
- Steps: Price $600 plus interest $60. → = $660.

**Question (choice, `borrow_test`):** Which question helps most in deciding?

- ✅ What does it cost in total, and what does waiting cost me?
- ◻️ Can I get approved?
  - *If picked:* Approval says the lender is willing, not that it's worth it.
- ◻️ What's the lowest monthly payment?
  - *If picked:* A low payment can hide a high total.

*Why:* Total cost against the cost of waiting is the heart of it.

**Card: Four questions**

> Many people ask: What's the total cost? Could I pay in a slow month? What happens if I wait or don't borrow? Does it last, or help me earn more? Comparing the APR or CAT lets you set offers side by side. These are questions, not a rule.

**Question (scenario, `borrow_test`):** Your bike, needed to get to work, breaks. A repair costs $200 and you have $50 saved. What do you do first?

- **Good:** Compare the total cost of borrowing, other ways to pay, and the cost of waiting (You see the whole picture before you choose.)
- **OK:** Ask a family member whether they could help, then decide (Another option to weigh, though the cost question remains.)
- **Poor:** Take the first loan offered (You haven't compared the cost or the alternatives.)

*Why:* Compare before you commit.

**Action** (`goals.debt`): See how long $1,000 at 24% takes to pay off, and how paying more changes it.

### Leer un estado de cuenta de tarjeta

*En la vida real:* Busca la fecha límite y el pago mínimo en un estado de cuenta real, tuyo o de un familiar.

**Tarjeta: Cuatro recuadros**

> Rafael abre el estado de cuenta de su tarjeta. Cuatro cosas importan primero: la fecha límite de pago, el pago para no generar intereses, el pago mínimo y el CAT. ¿Qué pago evita que le cobren intereses el siguiente periodo?

- ✅ El pago para no generar intereses, antes de la fecha límite
- ◻️ El mínimo, a tiempo
  - *Si la eliges:* El mínimo te mantiene al corriente, pero se siguen cobrando intereses sobre el resto.
- ◻️ Cualquier monto, pagado tarde
  - *Si la eliges:* Pagar tarde puede sumar comisiones e intereses.

*Por qué:* Ese monto está calculado para evitar intereses si se paga antes de la fecha límite.

**Tarjeta: El mínimo no es la meta**

> La CONDUSEF dice que la mayor parte de un pago mínimo se va en intereses. La fecha de corte cierra el periodo, y la fecha límite suele llegar unos 20 días después. El CAT muestra el costo anual de la tarjeta, con intereses y comisiones.

**Pregunta (number, `read_statement`):** Un saldo de $10,000 sin pagos por un año, al CAT típico de 40%. ¿Cuánto serían los intereses, más o menos?

- Respuesta: **4000**
- Pasos: 40% de $10,000. → 0.4 × $10,000 = $4,000.

**Pregunta (match, `read_statement`):** Une cada renglón con lo que te dice.

- Fecha límite de pago = El último día para pagar a tiempo
- Pago mínimo = Lo menos para estar al corriente, con intereses que siguen sumando
- CAT = El costo anual total de la tarjeta

*Por qué:* Cada renglón responde una pregunta distinta sobre el cobro.

**Acción** (`goals.debt`): Compara pagar el mínimo vs. pagar el doble en el saldo de una tarjeta.
