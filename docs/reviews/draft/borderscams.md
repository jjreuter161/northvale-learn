### Fake rates and fake jobs

*In real life:* Before you send money to get a rate or a job, ask: who gets paid first, and would a real one ask that?  
*4.1 min*

**Card: Too good to be true**

> A post offers $19.50 MXN per dollar when the fair rate is $18 MXN: send dollars to a personal account. Another offers a job in Texas if you first pay $300 for training. Both ask you to send money before anything arrives.

**Question (number, `fake_deals`):** At $19.50 MXN instead of $18 MXN, how many extra pesos would $1,000 bring?

- Answer: **1500**
- Steps: Extra per dollar: 19.5 − 18 = 1.5 pesos. → 1000 × 1.5 = 1500 pesos.

**Card: The fake check**

> A common job scam sends a check and asks you to deposit it and send part back. The check bounces later, and the bank wants its money back from you. The FTC says honest employers never ask you to pay to get a job.

**Question (tf, `fake_deals`):** If a deposited check shows up in your account, it's safe to send money back. → **false**

*Why:* A fake check can look fine at first. The bank can take the money back later.

**Question (match, `fake_deals`):** Match each offer to what it really is.

- Pay for training to get hired = A fee for a job: a warning sign
- Deposit a check, send some back = The fake-check scam
- A rate far above the fair one = A rate no real service could afford

*Why:* In each one, your money leaves first.
